Session 2: Introduction to PsychoPy

From Python in the console to stimuli on the screen

Welcome back! In Session 1 you learned to think in Python: variables, lists, loops, conditionals, and functions. That is all we need to program sophisticated experiments. For this session, we are going to work in the PsychoPy Coder on your own laptop. By the end of today, you will have built a working experiment that shows images in random order, collects keyboard responses, and gives feedback on every trial. Today, we change environment.

Learning outcomes

After this session, you can:

  • Open and close a PsychoPy window and explain the draw–flip cycle (prepare, draw, flip) that gives experiments their precise timing.
  • Present text and image stimuli (TextStim, ImageStim), for a fixed duration (core.wait()) or until a keypress (event.waitKeys()).
  • Load stimulus files dynamically with glob.glob() instead of hardcoding filenames.
  • Randomise trial order with random.shuffle() and explain why randomisation matters.
  • Collect and restrict keyboard responses (keyList=['left','right']) and use the response in your code.
  • Give conditional feedback: evaluate a response against a rule and show a success or failure image.

How today works

Today alternates between two modes. In short lecture segments we introduce each concept and demonstrate it live on the projector; in the exercises you put the concepts to work on your own, at your own pace, with the teaching team circulating. The chapters on this site follow the same order as the lectures, so everything shown at the front is here to re-read.

Before you start

  1. Get the materials. Download the workshop folder, unzip it, and keep everything together: your scripts must live next to the stimuli/ folder.
  2. Check your setup. Open the PsychoPy Coder, open materials/setup_check.py from the workshop folder, and press Run. You should see a window saying Your setup works! If you do not, ask for help (do not start the chapters with a broken install).
